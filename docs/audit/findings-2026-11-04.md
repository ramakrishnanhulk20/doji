## L-01 (Low): earned rewards cannot be claimed once the loan is in auction

**Where.** `LoanManager.sol` line 690, inside `_requirePassThrough` (lines 685 to 694): `if (state == LoanState.Auction) revert LoanInAuction(tokenId);`. `claimRewards` (lines 262 to 273) goes through it, with the borrower or the reward operator as caller. `PayDown.sol` line 248 calls `claimRewards`, so a harvest reverts the same way (C41 states this as intended).

**What goes wrong.** Rewards are not collateral, and a claim only moves tokens to the caller. It changes nothing the auction prices, sells or settles. Yet the claim is blocked for the whole life of an auction, which is also the only stretch in which the borrower can still cure the loan by repaying in full (C9). What was earned and not yet claimed when the auction starts has two endings:

- A buyer takes the position. Mezo's reward contracts pay the current owner of the tokenId at claim time (`swap-brief.md` section 4), so the buyer collects everything the borrower earned. The sale price and the borrower's surplus do not count it.
- Nobody buys and the keeper settles. `veBTC.withdraw` burns the NFT, `ownerOf` then returns the zero address, and the rewards sit in the reward contracts with nobody able to claim them. (This second ending is reasoned from the live source and from `MockVeBTC`, which copies mainnet's zero `ownerOf` for a missing id. I did not run it on a fork.)

The timing makes this systematic for the loans PayDown is meant to help. Maturities fall at Thursday 00:00 UTC, which is also the epoch flip, and Mezo pays an epoch's incentives only after the epoch ends (head chef's 4 Nov note in DECISIONS). The last epoch's rewards therefore become claimable at the instant the loan matures. A loan is overdue one second later, anyone may start its auction, and C48 runs every resolution job before any harvest, so on a keeper run the auction starts first and the harvest that would have used those rewards reverts.

**Exact sequence (the test's numbers, fixture price 80,000 MUSD per BTC).**
1. Alice deposits 1 BTC, borrows 30,000 MUSD due `m1` and owes 30,038.63. She names PayDown as reward operator.
2. Her position has earned 500 MUSD of pool fees, claimable at the flip.
3. At `m1 + 1` the loan is overdue. `startAuction` runs: start value 80,000, floor 56,000, 72 hours.
4. Alice's `claimRewards` and PayDown's `harvest` both revert `LoanInAuction(7)`.
5. A buyer pays the floor, 56,000. Alice's surplus is 24,459.44, the treasury gets 1,501.93, the market gets 30,038.63. The buyer then calls `claimFees` through PoolsVoter on the NFT and receives Alice's 500 MUSD, so the position cost the buyer 55,500. If nobody buys, the settle burns the NFT and the 500 MUSD is unclaimable.

**Impact in money.** The borrower loses everything earned and unclaimed at the moment of default. In this scenario that is 500 MUSD, 2.0% of Alice's 24,459.44 surplus, handed to the buyer. Lenders and the treasury are unaffected. The larger cost is the lost cure: if the rewards (or the rewards plus a BTC sale PayDown could make) cover the debt, one harvest during the auction repays in full and ends the auction (C9). Test 3 shows that path with rewards of 30,138.63 MUSD, larger than any real week of rewards, only to make the mechanism visible; real rewards would repay part of the debt.

**Smallest fix.** Let the claim through in an auction and keep every other pass-through banned there. `operatorOk` is true only for `claimRewards`, so:

```solidity
if (state == LoanState.Auction && !operatorOk) revert LoanInAuction(tokenId);
```

I applied exactly this to a copy of `src` in the scratchpad (not in the repo): all three tests pass, LoanManager grows from 24,193 to 24,204 bytes (372 under the limit), and the only existing tests that fail are the three that assert the old rule: `test_C4_PassThroughsRevertInAuction`, `test_C36_OperatorCannotClaimDuringAnAuction` and `test_C41_HarvestDuringAnAuctionReverts`. C4, C36 and C41 need the same edit. If the old rule is kept on purpose, C41 should say that earned rewards are not protected in an auction and the docs should tell borrowers to harvest before the due date.

**Test.** `packages/contracts/test/audit/RewardsStrandedInAuction.t.sol`, three tests. Fails today: `LoanInAuction(7)` (borrower claim), `L-01: the buyer collected rewards the borrower earned: 500000000000000000000 != 0`, `LoanInAuction(7)` (PayDown harvest). Not covered: Mezo's real reward contracts and `veBTC.withdraw` (fork), the settle ending, and the keeper's ordering.

## Info findings

**I-01. Yield books can sit a few wei above what the vault delivered.** `CreditMarkets._yieldDue` (lines 664 to 682) floors once per offer and booking interval. The vault floors once per change in CreditMarkets' balance. When an offer lives through several changes, Doji floors fewer times than the vault, so the sum of booked yield can exceed the vault's by dust. A 40-step random run (posts of both kinds, fills of one to three offers with duplicates, cancels, lumps, withdrawals, pull-outs and resumes) found booked yield plus paid 1 wei above the vault's total (5944923529298722221419 against 5944923529298722221418 wei; fuzz argument 301991613662269118657057496687877230414), within the first 20 runs of a zero-tolerance check. With a tolerance of `step / 4 + 1` wei, 30,000 runs found no larger gap. Effect: the last maker to withdraw can be short by those wei and `yieldOwedOf` never reaches zero. C31's cap already stops this from touching anyone's escrow. No test file, because it is wei-sized and has no fix worth the bytecode. Fix if wanted: let a maker with `stillOwed` under a few hundred wei clear it.

**I-02. No view says whether a parked offer is still in the vault.** `_offerYield` is private (line 81) and `Offer.parked` stays `true` after a pull-out, while the offer's MUSD is plain again from then on. A UI or keeper can compute it only from `OfferParked` and `PulledOut` event order and `parkingEra`. C50 asks the UI to show an offer's yield from a view, but the same offer's custody has none. Fix: a one-line view returning `_inVault(offerId)` (CreditMarkets has 2,774 bytes of headroom).

**I-03. `pullOut` is all or nothing and needs the vault to pay in full.** `pullOut` (lines 472 to 489) withdraws `parkedPrincipal` in one call and reverts `VaultShortfall` unless the vault burns exactly that and pays at least that. C52 says "the guardian's pull-out turns every parked offer plain", which is true only when the vault is healthy enough to pay everything. If Mezo moves the vault to a strategy with limited liquidity (the brief's section 8 shows the owner can do this with no delay, and Mezo's USDC vault already limits withdrawals), `pullOut` of the total reverts while single cancels below the limit still work. Fix: say so in C52, or add an optional amount so the guardian can pull in pieces (the era rule would then need a partial-era design, so documenting it is the cheaper answer).

**I-04. Strays that nobody can recover.** `CreditMarkets.rescueToken` refuses MUSD, BTC and sMUSD (line 499), so sMUSD above `parkedPrincipal` and MUSD above the solvency line stay forever (C25 chose this on purpose). `UnitMarket.rescueToken` refuses all MUSD (line 217) although only `treasuryOwed` is owed, so MUSD sent to it by mistake is stuck too. C46's wording ("never moves ... MUSD owed to anyone") suggests the surplus was meant to be movable. Fix for UnitMarket: allow MUSD above `treasuryOwed`.

**I-05. A MUSD-only harvest still reads the price.** `PayDown._start` calls `_price()` unconditionally (line 179), so an oracle outage blocks a harvest that would only repay with MUSD rewards and never sell. The borrower's own `claimRewards` and `repay` do not need the price, so nothing is trapped. Fix: read the price only when BTC is listed and an open loan needs it.

**I-06. Unlisted owner power, and one key for every role.** `CreditMarkets.setURI` (line 492) lets the owner change the ERC-1155 metadata URI; C19 says the admin surface is its listed setters plus two rescue functions. It cannot touch money, but wallets and marketplaces show that URI. Separately, `DeployCore` makes the deployer the owner, guardian and treasury of all four contracts (its own comment says so). That is a fine testnet setup. Before any mainnet use the owner should be a multisig and the guardian a separate key, and the standard should say so next to C20's rule about the keeper key.

## Attacking the standard: what section C misses

1. **C31 protects the pot, not the order of payment.** Makers are paid first come, first served from delivered minus paid. If booked yield ever runs ahead of delivered yield (I-01 at wei size, a vault index that reports more than it pays at any size), the makers who withdraw first are paid in full and the last are short, although each was owed a fair share. The standard should either say so, as it does for lenders ("makers short, never lenders"), or cap each maker at a pro-rata share.
2. **C32, C35 and C52 treat pull-out as the answer to a vault fault, but it needs the vault.** Nothing covers a vault that pays part or charges a fee (I-03). The standard should say what the guardian and the website do when `pullOut` reverts, which is: stop parking, and lenders cancel in pieces.
3. **No bound on how much principal one vault can hold.** F27 lets each lender choose, and the website switch is on by default, so most idle MUSD may sit in one account at one vault whose owner can swap the strategy or upgrade it with no delay (brief section 8). C35 limits the kind of damage a fault does, not its size. A `maxParkedPrincipal` set by the owner inside a hard cap, or a default of off for large offers, would bound it. This is Ram's call (F27).
4. **sMUSD is a bearer token in one account, and the vault trusts an ERC-2771 forwarder.** The 5 Oct veBTC incident was a sender-check flaw behind a forwarder. The sMUSD vault has the same pattern (brief section 10, item 8; the brief found no bypass). T9 names the vault's upgrade and index but not the forwarder. A flaw there would move every parked sMUSD at once, and every check in C30 to C35 assumes an honest vault.
5. **On testnet the yield can be sniped inside one transaction.** The vault's `pcv()` is unset on testnet and `PCV.distributeMUSD()` is open to anyone, so a parked post, a distribute and a cancel in one block takes a capital-weighted share of a lump (the brief measured +722.72 MUSD on 20,000 deposited, section 7 item 3). The non-goal about "deposits timed around the vault's yield lumps" assumes lumps arrive on their own schedule. On testnet the attacker also chooses the moment, so a tester can make the live yield figure look gamed. Reasoned from the brief; not run here.
6. **C4 and C41 do not say what happens to earned rewards when a position is sold or settled.** That is L-01. C48's ordering (resolution before harvest) turns it from an edge case into the usual case for an overdue loan.
7. **C39 and C40 give the borrower no way to say "never sell my BTC automatically".** The only switch is un-naming PayDown, which also stops MUSD repayment. A stranger can still trigger the sale of BTC rewards sized against the whole debt (the accepted non-goal); the cost is bounded per harvest by the allowance, and the sale is sized at the floor price, so when the pool trades at the oracle the contract sells up to the allowance (1% at deploy) more BTC than the debt needs and returns the extra as MUSD. Two operator modes (MUSD only, MUSD and BTC) would let a borrower choose.
8. **C40 and C7 together read the oracle for a harvest that does not need it** (I-05).
9. **C42's "keeps no balance between calls" is what makes `PayDown.rescueToken` safe to leave open.** The rescue can move any token, not only strays; it is safe only because it cannot run inside a harvest and nothing is held between calls. The standard should name that as the reason, so a later change that leaves a balance behind has to revisit the rescue.
10. **C35's sentence "a yield calculation never makes a principal movement revert" is stronger than the code.** `yieldIndex()` is read through try/catch, which catches a revert but not a reply that is too short to decode: the decode fails in CreditMarkets itself. DECISIONS already accepts this for the current era's fills and cancels; the sentence in C35 should say "a vault that answers with well-formed data".
11. **C16's "book, then cut, both before any outside call" is read loosely.** `_bookYield` reads the vault's index (a static call) before the cut. It cannot change state, so nothing is wrong, but the wording says "no outside call".
12. **C43 to C47 are upheld, with product limits worth a line.** A seller contract whose hook refuses units cannot cancel (stated non-goal); a `cancel(listingId, to)` that names the receiving address would remove that case. The par cap on price stops a seller from listing BTC-heavy units of a settled date above par. The receiver hook is non-reentrant, so a contract buyer cannot list the units it has just bought inside the same transaction. `setTreasury` hands fees already owed to the new treasury (stated in NatSpec, not in C44 or C46).
13. **C51 asks for a fork dry run of deposit and withdraw before deploy.** `DeployCore._preflightPeriphery` checks that the vault, router and factory have code and that the vault takes this MUSD; the 1:1 deposit smoke lives in `rehearse-wave2.sh`, which was untracked and being edited while I looked. I did not run it.
14. **C48 to C50 (keeper and web).** Not reviewed. The checks that matter most, if someone reviews them: the harvest job never lists BTC when MUSD rewards alone cover the debt; it skips positions in auction without sending; and nothing keys an offer or a listing by id alone across the two pairs.

## Checked and found sound

- **C30, exact vault movement.** `_parkInVault` approves exactly `amount`, resets the approval to zero and requires the sMUSD balance to rise by exactly `amount`; `_takeFromVault` requires an exact burn and at least `amount` of MUSD back. A short payer, a short minter, a refusing vault and an unreadable index each stop only parked paths (existing tests, and I read the code against them).
- **C31, yield paid only from yield delivered.** Delivered grows in three places only (`_parkInVault`, `_takeFromVault`, `_claimFromVault`) and only by measured MUSD arriving. `withdrawOfferYield` pays `min(owed, delivered - paid)`. MUSD or sMUSD sent unasked is never counted. A falling or absurdly large index books zero; the snapshot moves forward only.
- **Books against the vault.** A fuzz of my own (30,000 runs of 40 steps, three makers, three dates, one to three offers per fill with duplicates, plain and parked, cancels, lumps, withdrawals, pull-outs, resumes): booked yield plus paid never exceeded the vault's total by more than `step / 4 + 1` wei (I-01). I did not bound how far below it can fall.
- **C32, pull-out and eras.** One index read, no loop, `parkedPrincipal` set to zero, the next era started. An offer of an ended era never touches the vault again and books only up to the recorded index. Reading `_inVault`, `_yieldDue` and `_bookYield` for every state combination turned up no way for `parkedPrincipal`, `Offer.remaining` and the vault balance to disagree.
- **C35, plain paths never call the vault.** `postOffer`, a plain cancel and fill, `credit`, `creditBtc`, `finalize`, `redeem`, `withdrawCollected` and every LoanManager path other than a parked fill do not call the vault: `_yieldDue` returns before `_readIndex` for a plain offer.
- **Reentrancy.** Every CreditMarkets function that calls the vault is `nonReentrant`. The ERC-1155 hook in `claimUnits` runs under the lock and can reach neither CreditMarkets state nor LoanManager's paths into it. UnitMarket's hooks run after its books are final, and its own hook is non-reentrant.
- **Roll and borrow with parked offers.** A roll that takes a parked offer whose withdraw carries a yield surplus (the test the 4 Nov review said was missing): a 60,000 MUSD parked offer, a roll taking 31,000 of it against an old debt of 30,038.63; LoanManager received exactly the 31,000 principal and kept nothing, the borrower received the 961.37 above the old debt, 29,000 stayed parked, and the yield surplus stayed in CreditMarkets as delivered yield. Mixed fills with duplicate ids book each stretch once.
- **Reward operator.** `_requireBorrower(tokenId, operatorOk)` passes the operator only from `claimRewards`; `setRewardOperator`, `withdrawCollateral`, roll, borrow, vote, reset, rebase and extend call it with false. The operator lives in the position record and `delete _positions` clears it on withdraw, purchase, settle and write-off. `rescuePosition` works only on tokens with no record. Naming LoanManager reverts.
- **PassThrough.** `isReward` is read on the contract PoolsVoter registered for the gauge, per side; balances are measured before the claims and all read again before any payout; `msg.sender` is the operator or borrower through the delegatecall; direct calls to the library are harmless (it holds no state and PoolsVoter checks the caller). The raw token count (16) and the distinct count PayDown uses are consistent.
- **PayDown.** The borrower is read first and every leftover goes to that address; `_payOut` pays balance minus the pre-claim base, so strays stay; approvals to the Router and LoanManager are exact and reset; the BTC floor is enforced by the Router's minimum and again by PayDown's measured MUSD; `_btcCovering` rounds up at both steps and I checked the floor covers the need; a token listed twice is measured once; the borrower's swap list refuses MUSD, BTC, duplicates, zero minimums and bad routes; `harvestAndSwap` is borrower only; a hostile hop cannot re-enter and holds no allowance; nothing is left in PayDown after a harvest.
- **UnitMarket.** Listings come only from the fixed CreditMarkets, with the holder as seller and exactly 64 bytes of terms; batch and mint arrivals revert; price is above zero and at most par; a buy is exact, cost rounds up and fee rounds down, so a dust buy never costs zero (existing fuzz) and splitting a buy cannot cost less in total, because every part rounds up; the fee is snapshotted; cancel and return are never paused; `treasuryOwed` is always covered by the MUSD held.
- **Admin powers.** Every setter is bounded as C19 and C42 say (PayDown slippage 300 bps, UnitMarket fee 100 bps, the guardian can only pause or stop parking, only the owner unpauses PayDown and UnitMarket); renounce is disabled on all four; rescue cannot touch MUSD, BTC or sMUSD in CreditMarkets, veBTC in LoanManager, or units in UnitMarket. `setURI` is the only extra (I-06).
- **Size.** LoanManager runtime is 24,193 bytes, 383 under EIP-170; CreditMarkets 21,802; PayDown 11,287; UnitMarket 8,369.
- **The 20 Oct audit's tests** (`AuctionNeverReanchors`, `OwnerRetunesLiveLoanAuction`, `RollCashOutWhilePaused`) now pass; their fixes are in the base.
