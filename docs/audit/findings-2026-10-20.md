# Doji audit findings, 2026-10-20

Auditor: an independent review pass, separate from the build and read-only on src/ and script/ (not a third-party audit firm). Date: 2026-10-20. Code: working tree at `8ca2547` (contracts unchanged since the deployed `1a9bee7`). Standard: `docs/security/threat-model.md` section C, `docs/risk/parameters.md`.

Read in full, in the packet order: `LoanManager.sol`, `libraries/PassThrough.sol`, `CreditMarkets.sol`, `libraries/Resolution.sol`, `libraries/Quote.sol`, `libraries/LockReader.sol`, every file in `src/interfaces/`, `script/DeployCore.s.sol`, `packages/keeper/src/*.ts`, `.github/workflows/keeper.yml`. Tests read for coverage: `test/utils/*` (fixture and mocks), the unit suites' names and the relevant bodies (`Resolution.t.sol`, `Roll.t.sol`, `CreditInvariants.t.sol`).

No Critical or High issue was found. The custody, accounting and settle paths hold up. The one money issue that matters is in how an auction is priced after it starts.

## Summary

| Id | Severity | Title | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M-01 | Medium | An auction never re-prices upward, so after a crash and a rebound the market is paid less than the debt while the collateral covers it, and the borrower can buy their own position back below the debt | executed (2 failing tests) |
| L-01 | Low | The owner can change the floor, penalty and duration of an auction on a loan that is already open, including a floor below that loan's own line | executed (1 failing test) |

## M-01 (Medium): an auction never re-prices upward

**Where.** `packages/contracts/src/LoanManager.sol` lines 310 to 320 (`startAuction` fixes `startValue` and `floorPrice` once), lines 362 to 363 (`purchase` prices from that snapshot only; an overdue auction reads no price at all), `src/libraries/Resolution.sol` lines 31 to 33 (`floorPrice = max(start x floorBps, min(owed, start))`) and 44 to 51 (`priceAt`).

**What goes wrong.** When an auction starts with the collateral worth less than the debt (start value S below debt D), the floor is `min(D, S) = S` and the curve is flat at S. That price never changes. It stays on sale at S until settle, which needs the lock to end: up to about 3 weeks after maturity for a normal lock the borrower extended, and about 4 weeks for a permanent lock after `prepareSettle`. If Bitcoin recovers in that window, the first buyer pays S for a position worth more, and the market receives S instead of up to D.

- An LTV auction can only be cancelled once the loan is back under its 65% line. Between "worth more than the debt" (LTV below 100%) and "safe" (LTV at or below 65%) it keeps selling at S.
- An overdue auction can never be cancelled and `purchase` reads no price, so it sells at S whatever Bitcoin does.
- The borrower can be the buyer. Once an underwater auction exists, buying the position back at S always costs less than repaying D. A crash around maturity becomes a free option for the borrower: if Bitcoin keeps falling they walk away and lenders take the BTC at settle. If it recovers they buy back at S and lenders take the loss.

This breaks the consequence C29 states ("a loan whose collateral still covers its debt therefore never leaves a shortfall through an auction") and the F9 intent (a borrower must not buy their own position back below the debt while the collateral covers it). The C29 floor is met only against the stale start value.

**Exact sequence (test 1, LTV auction, borrower buys back).**
1. Alice deposits 1 BTC (80,000 MUSD at the fixture price) and borrows 30,000 MUSD due at m1. She owes 30,038.63.
2. The price falls until the debt is 125% of the collateral. Anyone calls `startAuction`. Start value 24,030.90, floor 24,030.90, flat.
3. One hour later the price recovers to 80% LTV. The collateral is worth 37,548.29, more than the debt, but still above the 65% line. `cancelAuction` reverts `StillUnsafe`.
4. Alice calls `purchase(TOKEN, max, now)` and pays 24,030.90. She owns the position again.

Result: the market collects 24,030.90 against a 30,038.63 debt. Lenders lose 6,007.73 MUSD (20% of the debt), and Alice keeps exactly that against repaying.

**Exact sequence (test 2, overdue auction).** Same loan. At m1 + 1 second the price is at 125% LTV and the overdue auction starts at 24,030.90. Ten days later Bitcoin has doubled from there: the collateral is worth 60,077.26, twice the debt. The lock ends at m3, so settle is not possible yet. Any buyer pays 24,030.90. The market is 6,007.73 short and the borrower's claimable is 0, although at that price a settle would have paid the market the full debt and the borrower about 28,500 in BTC.

**Impact in money.** Lenders lose `min(D, V at sale) - S` per affected loan, up to `D - S`. The risk study counts a lender loss only when Bitcoin stays below the debt until the sale or settle. This adds losses in the rebound cases the study counts as safe, so the study understates real losses. It needs a fall deep enough to put the loan underwater before the auction starts. At the deployed limits (40% borrow limit, 65% line) that means a gap of more than 35% between keeper runs, keeper downtime, or a crash at maturity. The study shows this has happened (12 March 2020, 39.5% in one day). When the start value is above the debt the same staleness costs the borrower instead (a buyer keeps any rise above the curve), but there the borrower can repay to stop it.

**Smallest fix.** Price every purchase against a fresh value and never below `min(D, fresh value)`:

```solidity
(,,, uint256 valueNow) = _collateral(tokenId);
paid = Math.max(_priceOf(auction), Math.min(owed, valueNow));
```

This adds an oracle read to overdue purchases. Settle already needs one (C7), so an outage stalls both, as it already stalls LTV auctions. A fuller fix would also let anyone restart an auction from a fresh value once the value rises a set percentage above the snapshot. That covers the borrower side as well.

**Test.** `packages/contracts/test/audit/AuctionNeverReanchors.t.sol`, `test_M01_BorrowerBuysBackBelowDebtAfterRecovery` and `test_M01_OverdueAuctionSellsAtStaleValueAfterBtcDoubles`. Both fail today: `C29: market paid less than min(debt, value) at sale: 24030904109589041095891 < 30038630136986301369864`. Not covered: real Mezo contracts. The existing `test_C29_UnderwaterSalePaysMarketTheStartValue` passes because it never moves the price after the auction starts.

## L-01 (Low): the owner can change auction terms on a loan that is already open

**Where.** `LoanManager.sol` lines 310 to 312: `startAuction` reads the global `auctionFloorBps`, `auctionDuration` and `penaltyBps` when the auction starts. Lines 832 to 844: `_setAuctionParams` checks the floor only against the global `liqLtvBps`, never against open loans' snapshotted lines. Lines 820 to 830: `_setRiskParams` lets the global line drop first.

**Exact sequence.**
1. Alice borrows 30,000 MUSD against 80,000 of collateral under a 65% line, a 70% floor, 72 hours and a 5% penalty.
2. The owner calls `setRiskParams(1_000, 4_900)` (allowed: 4,900 is below the 7,000 floor) and then `setAuctionParams(1_000, 5_000, 1 hours)` (allowed: 5,000 is above the new 4,900 line). Both calls are inside the C19 bounds.
3. Alice is one second late. The overdue auction starts on a 50% floor, a 10% penalty and a one-hour curve. Her loan's own line (6,500) is now above the floor (5,000), which `_setRiskParams` says must never happen.
4. One hour later a buyer pays 40,000.

**Impact in money.** Alice's claimable is 6,957.51 MUSD. Under the terms she borrowed with, the floor alone would have given her 24,459.44. She loses 17,501.93 MUSD, 22% of her collateral, and her time to react drops from 72 hours to one hour. Lenders are unaffected (C29 still pays the debt). The money moves from borrowers to the treasury and the buyer, who could be the owner. DECISIONS.md settled F7 with "per-loan snapshots of rate, liquidation line and auction terms", but the code snapshots the auction terms per auction only. The existing `test_C19_AuctionParamChangeDoesNotTouchRunningAuctionOrLiveLoan` changes these parameters with a live loan open, then checks only that loan's line.

**Smallest fix.** Snapshot `penaltyBps`, `auctionFloorBps` and `auctionDuration` into the `Loan` at origination and on roll. The unused `maxLtvBps` slot shows there is room. `startAuction` then reads the loan's copy, so the "line below floor" rule holds per loan.

**Test.** `packages/contracts/test/audit/OwnerRetunesLiveLoanAuction.t.sol`, `test_L01_OwnerCutsTheFloorAndDurationOfAnOpenLoan`. It fails today: `F7: auction terms changed on an open loan: 6957506849315068493150 < 24459438356164383561643`. Not covered: an owner acting beyond the hard-coded bounds (a non-goal).

## L-02 (Low): roll pays out new MUSD while borrows are paused

**Where.** `LoanManager.sol` lines 587 to 591: `_openLoan` skips the pause check for a roll. Lines 696 and 705: `_repayRolled` sends any principal above the old debt to the borrower.

**What goes wrong.** C19 makes the pause the only response to a Mezo change ("pause new risk and launch a new deployment"), and C19 keeps roll unpaused so honest borrowers can refinance. But a roll is not limited to refinancing. It can take a larger principal, up to maxLTV at the current value, and pay the excess out. So during a pause every open loan can still draw fresh MUSD. If the pause was triggered because valuation or custody cannot be trusted (a veBTC upgrade misreporting `locked()`, or a transfer flaw like the 2026-10-05 incident), borrowers can draw more debt against exactly the collateral in doubt, then lose or move the NFT. A later `writeOff` books zero recovery on that new debt.

**Exact sequence.**
1. Alice owes 30,038.63 at m1 against 1 BTC.
2. A veBTC change makes `locked()` report 2 BTC. The guardian calls `setBorrowsPaused(true)`. A fresh `borrow` from Bob reverts `BorrowsPaused`.
3. Alice calls `roll(TOKEN, m2, [offerM2], [60_000e18], 5_000, 0)`.

**Impact in money.** 29,961.37 MUSD leaves the protocol while paused, and the debt rises to 60,169.32 against a position really worth 80,000. Without a valuation fault the extra debt is bounded by maxLTV at a fresh price. The existing `test_C19_RollWorksWhileBorrowsPaused` already pays out about 961 MUSD of new principal while paused and treats it as expected.

**Smallest fix.** In `_repayRolled`, revert when `borrowsPaused && excess != 0`. Refinancing and top-up rolls keep working while paused, which is C19's reason for never pausing roll.

**Test.** `packages/contracts/test/audit/RollCashOutWhilePaused.t.sol`, `test_L02_RollTakesNewDebtWhileBorrowsArePaused`. It fails today: `C19: new MUSD left the protocol while borrows were paused: 29961369863013698630136 != 0`. Not covered: rolls that only refinance or top up, which should stay allowed.

## L-03 (Low): keeper loops are not bounded per run

**Where.** `packages/keeper/src/keeper.ts` lines 286 to 300: every run fetches every `Borrowed` event from `record.block` to head, then reads every token id ever borrowed. `packages/keeper/src/chain.ts` lines 191 to 206: one `getLogs` per 5,000 blocks, and any page that fails twice stops the whole pass. `.github/workflows/keeper.yml` line 27: `timeout-minutes: 10`.

**What goes wrong.** C20 says "its loops are bounded per run". Writes are bounded (`MAX_ACTIONS` at most 50), but reads grow without limit. Testnet makes about 18,000 blocks a day (4.8 s per block, from the docs brief), which is 3.6 log pages a day: about 110 pages after a month and 1,300 after a year. Each page is a public-RPC call with a 20-second timeout. One rate-limited page after its one retry ends the run. At roughly half a second per page, the scan alone passes the 10-minute job limit within the first year. Every scheduled run then times out, and no auction is started, cancelled or settled and no market is finalised by the hosted keeper. No contract state is harmed, and anyone can still call every function. Keeper uptime is a non-goal, but this code makes the outage certain over time, not best effort. DECISIONS.md already lists a cursor as a follow-up.

**Smallest fix.** Bound the scan. Start from `max(record.block, head - blocks in (max term + the C26 limit + a margin))`, since no loan can stay open longer than that unless the oracle is out. Or keep a cursor and the set of still-open token ids in the Actions cache. Either way, cap pages per run.

**Status.** Reasoned, not executed. It depends on block count and RPC latency over months. A Foundry test cannot show it, and the brief rules out running the keeper against the network.

## Attacking the standard: what section C misses

1. **No rule ties a sale to the value at the time of sale.** C11 and C29 are written against the start value only, and nothing limits how long an auction may sit at its floor before settle (up to about 4 weeks after the floor time). This is the root of M-01. Proposed text: "no purchase pays the market less than min(D, value at a fresh price at the time of purchase)".
2. **Auction terms are snapshotted per auction, not per loan.** C19 says per auction, while F7 and DECISIONS.md say per loan. C19 should snapshot floor, penalty and duration per loan and keep each loan's line below its own floor (L-01).
3. **The pause does not cover roll cash-outs.** C19 justifies never pausing roll as "it only takes offers already posted", but a roll can raise the debt and pay out. It should say "while paused, a roll may refinance or top up but may not pay out" (L-02).
4. **C20 is not upheld as written, and it misses two checks.** The scan is unbounded (L-03). C20 also says the keeper key has no role, but nothing enforces it. DeployCore makes the deployer owner, guardian and treasury, and the keeper reads its key from the same `.env`, so the keeper should refuse to run when its address is the owner, guardian or treasury. Separately, `workflow_dispatch` can run any pushed branch's code with `KEEPER_PRIVATE_KEY`. That is limited to collaborators, but it is still code that is not on the default branch. A `if: github.ref == 'refs/heads/main'` guard closes it.
5. **C26 bounds the time a loan can hold up a market, but not the cost.** One minimum loan (10 MUSD) on a permanent lock, voted just before maturity and left unpaid, keeps every lender at that date out of their money for about 5 weeks: 72 hours of auction, up to one epoch for the reset, then up to 4 weeks of lock. The only price is the penalty, taken from the attacker's own collateral. Partial redemption of collected MUSD before final, or a minimum loan size per date, would cap the harm.
6. **Irreversible preparation runs before maturity.** C11 allows `prepareSettle` while an LTV auction is unsafe before maturity. That resets the borrower's votes mid-epoch and permanently unlocks a permanent lock. If the loan then recovers and is cancelled, the borrower has lost the permanent lock (LoanManager never calls `lockPermanent`) and that epoch's vote rewards. The keeper does this at the first chance. Votes and boosts are a non-goal, but the standard should say whether this step waits until the loan is overdue.
7. **Nothing checks that the treasury can receive MUSD and BTC.** C19 lets the owner set any non-zero address. If MUSD refuses some recipients, as Liquity-style tokens refuse their own address and core system contracts, a bad treasury makes every purchase with a penalty revert. Auctions above the debt could then never sell. Reasoned only: I did not verify MUSD's recipient rules.
8. **`renounceOwnership` is inherited and not disabled.** After a renounce, stray tokens and positions can never be rescued, including the stranger-delivered BTC that the non-goals say the owner returns to the borrower. C19 lists the admin surface but does not mention it.
9. **Text that does not match the code (no money effect).** C15 allows redemption "by an operator that holder approved", but `redeem` only burns from `msg.sender`. C8 requires "loan caps" that do not exist, only `MAX_FILLS`. C12 says LoanManager "holds exactly its claimables" after settle, which is only true without stray BTC; the non-goal path leaves BTC above the totals.

## Checked and found sound

- C1 custody: `deposit` pulls from `msg.sender` with `transferFrom` and re-checks `ownerOf`. There is no `onERC721Received`, so `safeTransferFrom` into LoanManager reverts and a plain transfer never creates a position.
- C17 `lockAndDeposit`: an exact measured BTC pull, an exact allowance reset to zero, and `ownerOf` plus the C2 test before the id is recorded.
- C2: NORMAL escrow and no grant manager are checked at deposit, at lockAndDeposit and on every borrow and roll. Every resolution step needs custody, and `writeOff` only runs once custody is lost.
- C8 and C16 fill rules: the offer exists, the maturity matches exactly, the APR is at most the max, the amount is at most what remains, and the remainder is cut before quoting. No maker code runs, because units are recorded and minted later (C23).
- Quote: maker units plus treasury units equal the face exactly, with interest rounded up for the lender.
- C9 and C14 market counts: each fill adds one open loan, and each ending (repay, roll, purchase, settle, writeOff) removes exactly one. `credit` refuses final or empty markets, so the count cannot drift.
- C15: `finalize` needs maturity passed and zero open loans. Redemption snapshots are fixed, MUSD is capped at one per unit, and units burn before payment.
- C14 and C18 donations: every MUSD and BTC inflow is a measured exact pull, so stray tokens never reach an offer, a market or a claimable. No function is payable.
- C9 repay: anyone may pay, at most the amount owed is pulled, and the final payment deletes the loan and any auction in the same call.
- C11 cancel: only for auctions started at or before maturity, and only when a fresh price shows the loan safe. Overdue auctions can never be cancelled.
- C11 purchase: deadline and maxPay are enforced, an LTV auction needs an unsafe fresh price, the split sums to the price, and the NFT moves by `transferFrom`.
- C12 settle: floor time reached, overdue or unsafe, custody, a normal ended lock, no vote and a fresh price. The BTC split comes from the measured `withdraw` delta, and the split sums to what was received.
- C28 claimables: credited once, zeroed before payment, totals tracked. `rescueToken` cannot dip below the totals, and `rescuePosition` refuses any recorded position.
- C4 and C10 PassThrough: the tokenId acted on is LoanManager's checked one, and calldata is re-decoded with strict ABI rules. Reward contracts come from PoolsVoter, tokens must be whitelisted and are de-duplicated, balances are measured before and after, payouts happen after measuring, and all of it runs under LoanManager's lock.
- C5 vote cutoff: a normal lock cannot vote at or after `lockEnd - WEEK`, and `vote` and `extendLock` need an on-time, safe loan. `clearForSettle` resets only voters that hold a live vote, then unlocks.
- C10 `claimRebase`: the measured delta is credited to the borrower only.
- C19 setter bounds: maxLTV below the line, the line below the floor and at most 90%, penalty at most 10%, floor 50% to 95%, duration 1 hour to 7 days, max term at most 4 weeks. Ownership transfer is two-step.
- C27: both contracts fix each other in their constructors, with a mutual check and matching MUSD and BTC.
- C6 and C7: LockReader requires the exact word count and a positive amount, and treats a permanent lock as never expired. The price read uses try/catch, rejects zero and never uses a cached value.
- C26 time bound: a borrower cannot push the lock end after maturity (`extendLock` needs the loan on time), an overdue auction restarts at most once, and every path to settle ends within the C26 limit.
- C22 deploy preflight: code at every address, voter match, a secondary-voter guard, library code hash, lock layout probe, price range and the LoanManager address prediction are all checked.
- C20 keeper writes: every call is simulated, writes are capped, chain id and contract wiring are verified, and log text is sanitised. The workflow pins actions by SHA, uses a read-only token, has no pull-request trigger and runs no install scripts.
