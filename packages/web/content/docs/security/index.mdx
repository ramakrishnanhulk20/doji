Doji holds other people's veBTC positions. Custody comes first, because a contract that holds many positions concentrates one kind of risk: a single flaw can reach every one of them at once.

<Callout type="warn">
On 5 October 2026 an attacker moved 37 veBTC positions on Mezo mainnet through a trusted-forwarder (meta-transaction) sender-check flaw ([Mezo's incident report](https://mezo.org/blog/incident-report-october-5-2026)). Meta-transactions on veBTC are now disabled. Doji has no meta-transaction or forwarder path at all, and it names a flaw in veBTC itself as a non-goal: the guardian can stop new borrows, but cannot reverse a theft.
</Callout>

The full standard is [docs/security/threat-model.md](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md). It was written before any contract code, from a plain description of the system, and written again the same way for Wave 2 before any Wave 2 code and for the quests before any quest code. Section C is the definition of done: a contract is finished only when every rule is upheld and a test shows it. The contracts cannot be upgraded, so a bug or a Mezo change cannot be fixed in place; the only answer is to pause new risk and launch a new deployment that users move to themselves. That is what Wave 2 is: a new pair, deployed on 4 November 2026, that takes new offers and loans while the 20 October pair keeps serving what was opened on it.

## The rules, as the threat model groups them

C1 to C29 cover both pairs. The Wave 2 rules, C30 to C53, follow them, and the quest rules, C54 to C82, come last.

### Custody and identity

- C1. Only the address that owned a veBTC just before it entered LoanManager can become its borrower. A position pushed in any other way never becomes a position.
- C2. Only ordinary positions that no third party can lower or remove are accepted. If a position ever leaves custody, only a zero-recovery write-off runs for its loan, and repaying stays open.
- C3. LoanManager acts as owner only through the calls in the design: no veBTC approvals, no generic call, no merge, split or permanent lock, no meta-transactions. The treasury only ever receives fee units and penalties, and the penalties wait as claimables it collects itself. Ownership cannot be renounced.
- C4. Every borrower action passes one shared check that the caller is the recorded borrower. Pass-throughs never run during an auction, and `vote` and `extendLock` need an on-time, safe loan.
- C5. No vote in a normal lock's last week, so the reset settle needs is always available. `prepareSettle` clears votes on every voter that holds one.

### Valuation and terms

- C6. One function values collateral: locked BTC, never voting power, decoded for this chain's veBTC. A permanent lock is never expired.
- C7. One price: `fetchPrice()`, above zero. If it fails, borrow, roll, auction starts, purchase and settle all revert.
- C8. One quote turns principal, rate, time and fee share into the face and the units, and lender units plus protocol units equal the face exactly. Every borrow goes through the same checks.

### Resolution

- C9. A loan is Active, in Auction or Closed, and exactly one ending completes. Its date's open-loan count falls exactly once.
- C10. Reward forwarding pays the borrower exactly the measured increase of whitelisted tokens from gauges PoolsVoter registered, under a reentrancy lock.
- C11. A sale pays the market the smaller of the price and the debt, the treasury its penalty from what is left, and the borrower the rest. The price follows the snapshotted curve and reprices at a fresh value. Only an overdue or unsafe loan can be sold, prepared for settle or settled.
- C12. Settle runs only after an unsold auction past its floor time and the lock's end, never swaps, and splits the BTC actually received.
- C13. Anyone can start a resolution and no single party can stop one. Payouts are claimables, arrays are capped, and no on-chain loop runs over loans, offers, markets or holders.

### Credit markets

- C14. For each date, units issued equal the sum of its loans' faces, and collections grow only by measured arrivals. A donation changes nothing.
- C15. A date becomes final only after its Thursday with no open loans. Redemption and early payouts pay each unit the same total, never more than was collected.
- C16. An offer's maker, date and rate never change. Only the maker cancels, at any time. Only LoanManager fills.

### Funds in and out

- C17. Funds are pulled only from the caller. Repay pulls at most the debt. Locking leaves no BTC allowance behind.
- C18. No function is payable, and stray tokens never become anyone's claimable, collection or escrow.

### Governance and off-chain

- C19. The guardian can pause only new offers, new loans, lock creation and a roll's cash-out. Exits and resolutions are never paused. Settings stay inside hard bounds and are copied into each loan. Ownership moves in two steps.
- C20. The contracts re-check everything the keeper acts on. The keeper key holds gas only and refuses to run with a role, its workflow runs only on `main`, its loops are bounded, and a missed loan fails loudly.
- C21. The website reads values from the contract views, or computes them with the contracts' own formulas where no view exists, and simulates every write. It checks the chain, asks only for single-position or exact-amount approvals, sends on a retry only what the screen shows, reads every offer and open loan, and shows each loan's own auction terms.
- C22. A deploy fails unless every Mezo address has code, the lock layout and price check out, the two contracts point at each other, and the library's code hash matches.

### Credit-market specifics

- C23. No outside code runs inside a post, cancel, fill, borrow, roll, repay, auction start, purchase, settle or finalize, apart from Mezo's fixed contracts and the C10 reward contracts. Lenders mint their units later.
- C24. One clock: a date is a whole number of weeks, Thursday 00:00 UTC. Open before it, repay or roll without default up to it, overdue after it.
- C25. The fee is units at the same date, so the protocol shares any shortfall with lenders pro rata. CreditMarkets never rescues MUSD or BTC.
- C26. Every loan closes within a published time after its Thursday: the auction, one epoch for a reset, and the longest lock the position can carry.
- C27. CreditMarkets accepts fills and credits only from the LoanManager fixed in its constructor, and LoanManager talks only to its own CreditMarkets. There is no setter.
- C28. LoanManager's MUSD and BTC always cover the recorded claimables. Each is credited once and paid once, to its owner.
- C29. No purchase pays the market less than the smaller of the debt and the collateral's value at the time of sale.

### General standards

1. Primitives over lists: one predicate for a valid date, one positive test for collateral, one shared check for authorisation and for pause.
2. Normalize before compare: one lock decoder, one value, one price, one quote, one clock, one BTC reader.
3. Validate outputs like inputs: units are minted only as debt backs them, credits only for funds measured arriving.
4. Fail closed: revert on an unknown lock layout, a zero or stale price, a bad date, a short fill or a custody mismatch. Exits and resolutions are the deliberate exception and are never paused.
5. Name the non-goals, below.

## The Wave 2 rules, by feature

Each rule links to its full text in the threat model.

### Offers that earn in sMUSD while they wait

- [C30](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c30). Parked principal is exactly accounted for and never topped up from anyone else: a deposit must mint exactly the amount, a withdraw must burn exactly the principal and return at least that much, and a borrower is paid exactly the principal.
- [C31](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c31). Makers are paid yield only from yield the vault actually delivered, never from escrow or collections. If booked yield ever runs ahead of delivered, by a few wei of rounding or a vault that reports more than it pays, the last makers to withdraw are short, never lenders.
- [C32](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c32). An offer is parked or plain from the moment it is posted, and its yield clock stops for good when its MUSD leaves the vault. The guardian's pull-out runs in fixed gas with no loop.
- [C33](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c33). Fills and cancels book yield and cut the remainder before the vault is called, under the reentrancy lock.
- [C34](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c34). No standing permission reaches parked money: the vault is approved for exactly the deposit and the approval is reset, and rescue never moves sMUSD.
- [C35](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c35). A vault fault stays inside parked principal. Plain offers, repay, redemption, early payouts, finalize and every resolution never call the vault, and every keeper run checks that the vault would return the whole parked principal.

### The reward operator

- [C36](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c36). Only the borrower names or clears the operator, which can call `claimRewards` for that position and nothing else, and it is deleted with the position.
- [C37](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c37). Rewards go to whoever calls the claim, and naming an operator never takes away the borrower's own claim, clear or repay. LoanManager relies on no PayDown code.

### PayDown

- [C38](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c38). A harvest moves only what it received, inside one call that cannot be re-entered, and everything not repaid goes to the borrower in the same call.
- [C39](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c39). No harvest sells BTC below the oracle price less the slippage allowance (1% now, never above 3%), checked on the MUSD measured arriving. A token with no reference price is sold only in a harvest the borrower runs, at the borrower's own minimum.
- [C40](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c40). A harvest sells only what the debt needs, and with no loan it sells nothing.
- [C41](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c41). A harvest changes a loan only by repaying it, also during an auction, so a harvest that covers the debt cures it, and it reverts whole if any step fails.
- [C42](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c42). PayDown's addresses are fixed at deployment. The owner can set only the capped slippage allowance, the guardian and an unpause, and rescue strays; the guardian can only pause harvests, which never touches the borrower's own claim or repay.

### UnitMarket

- [C43](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c43). A listing exists only for real units of the Wave 2 CreditMarkets, created by the holder's own transfer, so no approval for all units is ever needed.
- [C44](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c44). A listing's terms, its fee rate included, never change after it is posted, and ids are never reused.
- [C45](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c45). A buy is exact and paid in full: never a part fill, never at zero cost, never above the buyer's maximum price.
- [C46](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c46). Exits are never blocked: the seller can cancel at any time and anyone can return an expired listing, whatever the pause or fee.
- [C47](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c47). A listing price is the seller's ask and never a value: no contract, keeper or screen uses it as the worth of a unit or of collateral.

### The keeper, the website and the two pairs

- [C48](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c48). Every resolution job runs before any harvest, harvests have their own capped budget, and every harvest is simulated before it is sent.
- [C49](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c49). Every offer, loan, unit, date, listing and harvest is identified by its contract and its id, never by the id alone, and every write goes to the pair that holds the object.
- [C50](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c50). The website shows an offer's yield and whether its MUSD is in the vault from contract views, names only the address-book PayDown, and asks for an exact allowance for a buy.
- [C51](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c51). The deploy checked the vault, the Router and every fixed address before sending anything.
- [C52](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c52). During a vault outage the website builds borrows and rolls from plain offers only. If the guardian's pull-out cannot run, parking stops and lenders cancel parked offers one by one.
- [C53](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c53). No write sends a bound looser than the one on screen: a re-read quote, minimum or price goes out as the tighter of the fresh value and the one the user saw.

Wave 2 also changes a few earlier rules: the borrower check (C4) now lets the operator claim, and a claim runs during an auction; a reward claim (C10) pays the caller; the guardian (C19) can also stop parking and pull out; and CreditMarkets never rescues sMUSD (C25). The [threat model](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md) lists every change.

## The quest rules, by feature

Each rule links to its full text in the threat model. No contract, keeper step or faucet limit reads EXP, so a wrong EXP cannot move money (C71). The Faucet had the reviewer's threat pass and the test suite, not a separate audit. C81's [load check](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/proof/load-check-2026-11-06.md) ran on a fork on 6 November 2026. C82's checks after a production deploy ran on 11 November 2026: a made-up token was refused and one real claim landed, recorded on the [proof page](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/proof/quests-2026-11-06.md#the-production-site-11-november-2026).

### The Faucet

- [C54](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c54). A claim is one whole kit, to one address, once ever, and only the relayer can call it. Every record is written before any value moves, and a failed payment reverts the whole claim.
- [C55](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c55). Recipient code has a fixed price: the BTC payment forwards a fixed gas allowance and copies no return data. There is no is-it-a-contract check, because that would block smart wallets.
- [C56](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c56). The Faucet's views tell the truth and never revert. One function turns block time into the UTC day, for `claim` and the views alike.
- [C57](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c57). No burst can take a whole day: claims so far today never pass what has been released so far.
- [C58](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c58). The owner's reach is bounded and the Faucet owes nobody. Setters stay under hard-coded caps, pause stops `claim` only, withdraw pays only the owner, and BTC is read one way.

### The claim route

- [C59](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c59). The route can sign one shape of transaction: `claim` of a parsed address, to the address-book Faucet, on chain 31611, at value zero, under fixed gas limits. Nothing in it comes from the request.
- [C60](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c60). No verified token, no signature. Every path to the signer passes one Cloudflare check, and any doubt is a refusal.
- [C61](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c61). The address sent to Cloudflare is only the one the platform sets. Doji stores no IP and logs no IP or token.
- [C62](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c62). Only a transaction that simulates gets sent. The other reads choose the refusal message and never permit a send.
- [C63](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c63). Wasted relayer gas is bounded with no database: a small fixed number in flight, read from the chain, and the loser of a race gets a refusal, never a hash.
- [C64](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c64). Input is checked cheap-first and the reply is a closed list. The browser shows success only when the chain confirms it.
- [C65](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c65). Keys stay in one place and do one job: production only, on the server only, and a stolen relayer key reaches one day's claims until the owner replaces it.

### The quest engine

- [C66](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c66). One address parser for every source, so one wallet has one row and one total however it is spelled.
- [C67](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c67). Only Doji's contracts can earn EXP. A look-alike contract or an old-pair log earns nothing.
- [C68](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c68). One quest table. A wallet comes from the event's own field and never from the transaction's sender, and a quest is decided from logs alone.
- [C69](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c69). One EXP function for the browser, the leaderboard and the image. Each quest counts once per wallet, and anyone can rebuild a wallet's EXP from the repository and the chain.
- [C70](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c70). An incomplete read is never shown as a final number.
- [C71](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c71). EXP decides nothing today. Every page and image says no more than the published sentence.
- [C72](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c72). Outbound calls have a fixed shape: constant hosts, no redirects, and caps on size, time and pages.

### The leaderboard and the bonus file

- [C73](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c73). Public routes do fixed work. No volume or variety of requests raises upstream calls above a fixed rate, and an address has one URL.
- [C74](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c74). A bad bonus file fails the build and is never partly applied. An entry is added only with the reporter's yes.
- [C75](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c75). Doji's wallets are one list that the rankings remove. It covers Doji's own keys, not a friend's wallet.

### The share page and image

- [C76](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c76). An address from a URL is only ever displayed. Every button acts for the connected wallet alone.
- [C77](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c77). The image and metadata carry only values this code computed. Raw URL text never reaches them.
- [C78](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c78). Upstream values are checked as input to the page, other sites cannot frame any page, and Cloudflare's widget loads only when a person reaches the claim step.

### Phone wallets

- [C79](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c79). Phone wallets get the same rules as browser wallets. The account and chain are checked before every write, and connecting and claiming ask for no signature.

### Seed wallets, load and deploy

- [C80](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c80). Seed wallets are ordinary users with no role. The site warns before a tester lends at a date that holds a seed loan, and seed loans carry collateral above their debt.
- [C81](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c81). Earlier limits are rechecked at quest volume: 300 offers, 300 loans and 100 live auctions leave the keeper's bounds and the app's reads holding, and a run past a bound turns red.
- [C82](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/threat-model.md#c82). Deploy checks: the Faucet's code, MUSD, amounts and relayer are checked, and each production deploy must refuse a made-up token and land one real claim.

## Static analysis

Slither 0.11.6 on the Wave 2 code found 57 results at the deploy gate, and a wider run that also covers Doji's own libraries finds 13 more. The Faucet adds 12, so on 6 November 2026 the same two runs read 69 and 82. Every one, with where it is and why it is safe, is in [docs/security/slither-2026-11-04.md](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/security/slither-2026-11-04.md).

## What Doji does not defend against

- A malicious or broken Mezo contract or upgrade: veBTC, PoolsVoter, BoostVoter, the fee distributor, MUSD or PriceFeed. A veBTC flaw reaches all collateral at once.
- Wrong prices inside the oracle's 60-second window, MUSD trading away from 1 USD, or an oracle outage longer than the time limit.
- Bitcoin price moves after a settle. Lenders paid in BTC carry that risk until they sell.
- Losses shared across a date. Every unit at that date shares a shortfall, including units of lenders who never chose that loan.
- A unit buyer's risks. Wave 2 units can be listed on UnitMarket at a price of at most 1 MUSD a unit, but UnitMarket gives no price, no buyer and no guarantee, and a buyer takes on that date's shared losses. 20 October units can be sent but not listed.
- Interest after the date. The face is fixed while a late loan resolves.
- Mempool griefing that only makes a borrow revert, such as a cancel or another fill landing first.
- Keeper uptime. The hosted keeper is best effort, and anyone can run it.
- The value of votes, incentives and boosts, none of which counts as collateral. Rewards a third party pays straight to LoanManager are credited to nobody.
- Mezo's PoolsVoter trusts an ERC-2771 forwarder. A flaw there could act as LoanManager on PoolsVoter; the worst outcome is a delayed settle.
- A secondary voter Mezo adds to veBTC after deployment, whose votes would block settle until reset by other means.
- BTC that a stranger's fee-distributor claim delivers for an expired lock is credited to nobody. The owner can move it with `rescueToken` and is trusted to return it to that position's borrower.
- A reward token whose own code calls out during a claim (on Wave 2, any token the gauge's reward contract lists; on the 20 October pair, a whitelisted one). At most it can move value that is credited to nobody.
- A compromised wallet or RPC, or an npm or Vercel supply-chain attack beyond lockfile pinning.
- An owner key compromise beyond the hard bounds, or a settings change inside them that lands just before your borrow or roll. The setters have no timelock; the site shows the terms a loan will get.
- A delay, not a loss, on the share of a date still owed by an unpaid loan, up to about 5 weeks. Everything already collected can be withdrawn from the date.

Added for Wave 2:

- A failure, loss, pause, fee or upgrade in Mezo's sMUSD vault. While it refuses withdrawals, parked offers cannot be cancelled or filled, and a pull-out also needs it to work. The vault trusts an ERC-2771 forwarder, and a flaw there would reach every parked dollar at once. A plain offer is the choice that nothing outside Doji can block.
- No cap on the total parked in one vault, and deposits timed around the vault's yield payments, which dilute every holder equally as the vault is designed. On testnet the vault has no fee collector wired, so anyone can trigger a payment and take a share of it inside one transaction.
- Price moves and ordering profits inside PayDown's slippage allowance and the oracle's window, thin pools (a harvest that cannot meet its floor reverts and waits), and reward tokens with no reference price, which are sold only when the borrower runs the harvest with their own minimum.
- A borrower who names a hostile operator: they lose that position's rewards, never its collateral or debt. A borrower who wants MUSD-only pay-down without BTC sales has no switch for it today, other than naming no operator.
- One key holding every role at deployment. That is a testnet setup: before any mainnet use the owner becomes a multisig and the guardian a separate key.

Added for the quests:

- One person using many wallets. Turnstile, one claim per address and the daily limit slow that down and do not prove one human. A paid solving service can still keep claims scarce.
- A claim for an address the caller does not own, which only gives that address a kit.
- Two wallets of one person completing "a stranger borrows from your offer" or trading units between them, and dust actions, which count unless a quest says otherwise.
- A lying explorer or RPC changing the EXP shown, which is why EXP decides nothing today. An explorer outage makes quests and the rankings unavailable.
- A stolen relayer key within its limit, an owner key compromise, which reaches the whole Faucet, and the Faucet running out through honest use.
- Reuse of the WalletConnect project ID by a look-alike site, since the ID is public and open to any origin. A wallet's own prompt shows which site is asking.
- A compromise of Cloudflare, Reown, Vercel, GitHub or the wallet app beyond lockfile pinning, and a wallet that hides what it signs.
- Screenshots, fake images and vanity addresses. A wallet's page shows the full address.
- Legal terms for any future use of EXP, which do not exist yet.
