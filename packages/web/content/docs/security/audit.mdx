Doji's code has had two independent review passes, one for each pair. Neither was a third-party audit firm, and no outside firm has reviewed Doji's code. Doji runs on Mezo testnet only.

The Faucet, added on 5 November 2026 for the [quests](/docs/getting-started/quests), had no separate audit. It had the reviewer's threat pass and the test suite, and it holds test tokens only.

## 4 November 2026: the Wave 2 code

<Callout type="info">
This was an independent review pass, separate from the build and read-only on the contracts and scripts. It was not a third-party audit firm. The full report is [docs/audit/findings-2026-11-04.md](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/audit/findings-2026-11-04.md).
</Callout>

The review read CreditMarkets, LoanManager, PassThrough, PayDown and UnitMarket and their interfaces in full, diffed against the 20 October code, with the threat model's rules C1 to C52 as the standard. It ran the unit suite, then deeper random runs than the defaults (1,500 invariant runs of depth 150 on the credit accounting, and 10,000 fuzz runs on the Wave 2 suites), and a 30,000-run fuzz of its own on the yield books. It did not run the fork tests and did not review the keeper or the website. It found no Critical, High or Medium issue: one Low and six Informational. Its summary: every vault amount is judged by a measured balance change, yield can only be paid out of yield that arrived, PayDown keeps no balance between calls, and UnitMarket moves each token after its own books are final.

A harvest that covers the debt now repays it and ends the auction. | [RewardsStrandedInAuction.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/audit/RewardsStrandedInAuction.t.sol): three tests that failed on the reviewed code and pass on the deployed code |

### Wave 2 L-01 in numbers

In the review's test, Alice deposits 1 BTC, borrows 30,000 MUSD and owes 30,038.63 on a Thursday, and names PayDown as her reward operator. Her position earns 500 MUSD of pool fees, which Mezo pays only once the epoch ends, and epochs end at Thursday 00:00 UTC, the same moment the loan falls due. One second later the loan is overdue and its auction starts. On the reviewed code her own claim and PayDown's harvest both reverted, a buyer paid the floor of 56,000, and then collected her 500 MUSD, 2.0% of her 24,459.44 surplus. On the deployed code she, or PayDown for her, claims during the auction, and a harvest whose rewards cover the debt repays it and ends the auction.

### What the review said the standard missed

The review also attacked the threat model and named 14 gaps. These changed the standard:

1. C31 now says makers are paid first come, first served from yield delivered, so if booked yield ever runs ahead the last makers are short, never lenders.
2. C52 now says what happens when the guardian's pull-out cannot run (I-03).
3. C35 now promises that a yield calculation never blocks a principal movement only for a vault that answers with well-formed data.
4. C42 now names the reason PayDown's open rescue is safe: PayDown keeps no balance between calls, and the rescue cannot run inside a harvest.
5. C46 now says that changing the treasury hands over fees already owed.
6. The vault's trust boundary (T9) now names its ERC-2771 forwarder, and three non-goals were added: no cap on the total parked in one vault, yield timed inside one transaction on testnet, and no MUSD-only pay-down mode.
7. The L-01 fix changed C4, C36 and C41 (F37).

The keeper and website rules, C48 to C50, were outside this review.

### Checked and found sound in Wave 2

Exact vault movement (C30), yield paid only out of yield delivered (C31), the pull-out and its parking eras (C32), plain paths that never call the vault (C35), reentrancy around every vault call and receiver hook, rolls and borrows that take parked offers, the reward operator's reach, PassThrough's `isReward` check, PayDown's floors and its empty balance after every harvest, UnitMarket's exact buys and always-open exits, every owner and guardian power and its cap, and the contract sizes, all under the 24,576-byte limit. The 20 October review's three regression tests still pass. The report lists each with what was checked.

## 20 October 2026: the first contracts

<Callout type="info">
This was an independent review pass, separate from the build and read-only on the contracts and scripts. It was not a third-party audit firm. The full report is [docs/audit/findings-2026-10-20.md](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/audit/findings-2026-10-20.md).
</Callout>

The review read every contract, library, interface, the deploy script, the keeper and its workflow, against the threat model's section C and the risk study. It found no Critical or High issue: one Medium and three Low. The custody, accounting and settle paths held up. It ran the unit suite and wrote a failing test for each finding it could execute; it did not run the fork tests. The fixes were deployed in the 20 October contracts, together with code this review never read: [Code added with the fixes](#code-added-with-the-fixes) says who checked that code and how.

### Findings

| Id | Severity | What went wrong | How it was fixed | Proven by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M-01 | Medium | An auction never repriced upward. After a crash and a rebound, the market could be paid less than the debt while the collateral covered it, and the borrower could buy their own position back below the debt. | Every purchase reads a fresh price and pays the larger of the curve and the floor recomputed at today's value. Overdue auctions read the oracle too. | [AuctionNeverReanchors.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/audit/AuctionNeverReanchors.t.sol) and the M01 tests in [AuditFixes.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/unit/AuditFixes.t.sol), including a fuzz test |
| L-01 | Low | The owner could change the floor, penalty and length of the auction of a loan already open, inside the hard bounds. | Each loan copies its penalty, floor and duration when it opens or rolls, and its auction reads the loan's copy. | [OwnerRetunesLiveLoanAuction.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/audit/OwnerRetunesLiveLoanAuction.t.sol) and `test_L01_OpenLoanKeepsItsAuctionTerms` |
| L-02 | Low | A roll could pay out new MUSD while new borrows were paused. | While paused, a roll that would pay anything out reverts. Refinancing and top-up rolls still work. | [RollCashOutWhilePaused.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/audit/RollCashOutWhilePaused.t.sol) and the two L02 tests in AuditFixes.t.sol |
| L-03 | Low | The keeper rescanned every loan since deployment on each run, so it would outgrow its 10-minute job over time. Reasoned, not executed. | Each pass is bounded: explorer API first, at most 64 RPC pages, 150 open loans, 32 dates and 20 explorer calls per event. A date with more open loans on chain than the scan found fails the run loudly. | The bounds in [the keeper](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/keeper/README.md). ### M-01 in numbers

In the review's test, a borrower owed 30,038.63 MUSD. The price fell until the debt was 125% of the collateral and an auction started flat at 24,030.90. An hour later Bitcoin recovered to 80% LTV: the collateral was worth 37,548.29, more than the debt, but still above the 65% line, so the auction could not be cancelled. The borrower bought the position back for 24,030.90. Lenders lost 6,007.73 MUSD, 20% of the debt. With the fix, the same purchase pays at least the debt, because the collateral covers it at the moment of sale.

### L-01 in numbers

The owner lowered the line to 49% and then set a 50% floor, a 10% penalty and a one-hour auction. A loan opened under 65%, 70%, 5% and 72 hours went overdue by one second and sold an hour later. The borrower's share fell from 24,459.44 MUSD under the loan's own terms to 6,957.51. Lenders were unaffected; the money moved to the treasury and the buyer. With the fix, the loan keeps the terms it opened with.

### Gaps in the standard, and how each was answered

The review also attacked the threat model itself.

1. No rule tied a sale to the value at the time of sale. C11 and C29 now require every purchase to reprice at a fresh value (M-01).
2. Auction terms were copied per auction, not per loan. C19 now copies them per loan (L-01).
3. The pause did not cover a roll's cash-out. C19 now says a paused roll may refinance or top up but not pay out (L-02).
4. The keeper's scan was unbounded, nothing stopped its key from holding a role, and a manual run could use code from any branch. The keeper now bounds every loop, refuses to run when its key is the owner, pending owner, guardian or treasury, and its workflow runs only on `main` (C20).
5. The time limit bounded how long one unpaid loan could hold up a date, but not the cost: one 10 MUSD loan could keep every lender at that date waiting about 5 weeks. Lenders can now withdraw what has been collected from the Thursday on ([Early payouts](/docs/concepts/early-payouts)), proven in [EarlyPayouts.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/unit/EarlyPayouts.t.sol).
6. `prepareSettle` could run before the Thursday on an unsafe auction past its floor time, and is not undone if the loan recovers. The standard now says this is deliberate and why: the borrower had the whole auction to repay (C11).
7. A treasury address that refuses MUSD or BTC could block every sale and settle. Penalties now wait as claimables the treasury collects itself (C3, C28), proven by the treasury tests in AuditFixes.t.sol.
8. `renounceOwnership` was inherited and not disabled. Both contracts now always revert `RenounceDisabled`, so the rescue paths always keep an owner (C19). It is attack 12 on [Try to break it](/docs/security/try-to-break-it).
9. Three lines of text did not match the code (in C8, C12 and C15). They were corrected.

### Checked and found sound

Custody and identity, the collateral test, the fill rules, the quote (lender units plus protocol units equal the face exactly), the open-loan counts, finalisation and redemption, donations, repay, cancel, purchase and settle conditions, claimables, the pass-throughs, the vote cutoff, the setter bounds, the two contracts' mutual wiring, the lock reader and price, the time limit, the deploy checks and the keeper's writes. The report lists each one with what was checked.

### Code added with the fixes

The commit that fixed these findings, [09e20c3](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/commit/09e20c3), also added code this review never read: early payouts (`withdrawCollected`, the answer to gap 5), purchase prices recomputed at today's value, each loan keeping its own auction terms, and penalties waiting as treasury claimables. Two more review passes read it before it was deployed. The first read the fixes: its one important finding, the keeper's list of open loans, was fixed, and two design questions were settled (who gains when Bitcoin recovers during a sale, and whether to build early payouts). The second read only the early payout code and the keeper fix and found no important issue: no order of calls pays a date more than it collected, pays a holder more per unit, or locks a holder out (C14, C15, C25).

What proves it:

- [EarlyPayouts.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/unit/EarlyPayouts.t.sol) runs 1,000 random cases of a lender who withdraws early against one who waits, and 1,000 of one lender who mixes both: part of its units paid early, the rest redeemed after the date is finalized, while loans are repaid, sold, settled in BTC or written off in between. No holder gets more than its share of what the date collected, no unit is paid twice, and only rounding dust is left. Two deliberately broken versions of `withdrawCollected`, one that forgets what a Claim was already paid and one that does not burn the units it takes, both fail it.
- [CreditInvariants.t.sol](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/test/unit/CreditInvariants.t.sol) runs random sequences of early withdrawals, redemptions, sales and settles across three dates and checks after every call that no date has paid out more MUSD or BTC than it collected.
- On testnet the 22 October date went through this order for real: an early payout of 28.51 MUSD, then a settle that added 0.00035461 BTC, then finalize. On 4 November 2026 the lender's next withdrawal paid exactly the 0.000354608 BTC the settle had added, and nothing more ([live paths, 4 November](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/proof/live-paths-2026-11-04.md)).

None of these passes was a third-party audit firm, and no outside firm has reviewed Doji's code. Doji runs on Mezo testnet only.
