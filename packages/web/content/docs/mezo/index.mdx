title: How Doji uses Mezo
description: Every Mezo contract Doji calls, the exact functions, why each matters, and the testnet and mainnet addresses.
---

Doji is built on Mezo's own pieces. The collateral is veBTC, the loan is MUSD, the price is MUSD's own price feed, and the borrower's votes and fees keep flowing through Mezo's voters and fee distributor. Since Wave 2, a lender's waiting MUSD can earn in Mezo's sMUSD savings vault, and a borrower's pool rewards can repay the loan through Mezo's own pools and Router. Take Mezo away and nothing is left.

## Every Mezo contract Doji calls

Testnet addresses are the ones in Doji's deployment records: [31611-wave2.json](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/deployments/31611-wave2.json) for the Wave 2 pair, which takes every new offer and loan, and [31611.json](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/packages/contracts/deployments/31611.json) for the 20 October pair, which keeps serving what was opened on it. Mainnet addresses are Mezo's live mainnet contracts, checked on Mezo's explorer and chain, and the [fork tests](/docs/developers/run-the-proof) run Doji against them.

## Mezo rules Doji is built around

- Weekly epochs turn on Thursday 00:00 UTC, and veBTC lock ends fall on the same boundaries. That is why every Doji loan is due on a Thursday.
- A veBTC lock lasts at most 28 days, so no Doji loan runs longer than 4 weeks, permanent lock or not.
- Votes persist from epoch to epoch. Each position gets one vote or reset per epoch, none in an epoch's first hour, and a lock with a standing vote cannot be withdrawn. Doji blocks votes in a normal lock's last week, so the reset settle needs is always possible.
- `locked()` returns three words on testnet's older veBTC and four on mainnet (with a boost). One reader decodes it for the chain it was deployed on and refuses anything else.
- Mezo pays an epoch's pool incentives only once the epoch has ended, at the same Thursday 00:00 UTC a loan falls due. That is why, on the Wave 2 pair, a reward claim and a PayDown harvest still run while a loan is in auction: the last week's rewards stay with the borrower, and a harvest that covers the debt ends the auction.

## What Doji never touches

LoanManager acts on positions only through the calls in the table. It gives no veBTC approval or approval-for-all, has no generic call or multicall, never merges, splits or makes a lock permanent, and never sells collateral on a DEX. The only sale anywhere in Doji is PayDown's sale of a position's rewards for MUSD, on Mezo's own pools, under the oracle floor. CreditMarkets gives the sMUSD vault no standing approval, and PayDown's approvals to the Router are exact and reset after each sale. Doji accepts no meta-transactions and has no forwarder: every call acts for the address that sent it.

<Callout type="warn">
On 5 October 2026 an attacker moved 37 veBTC positions on Mezo mainnet through a flaw in how a trusted forwarder (Mezo's meta-transaction path) passed on the sender. Mezo has since turned meta-transactions on veBTC off, and live veBTC's `forwarder()` returns the zero address ([Mezo's incident report](https://mezo.org/blog/incident-report-october-5-2026)). A contract that holds many positions concentrates exactly that kind of risk, which is why custody comes first in Doji's [threat model](/docs/security). Two non-goals remain and are stated. Mezo's PoolsVoter still trusts an ERC-2771 forwarder: a flaw there could act as LoanManager on PoolsVoter (vote, reset, claim), rewards would still land with LoanManager, and the worst outcome is a delayed settle. Mezo's sMUSD vault trusts one too, and a flaw there would reach every parked dollar at once, which is why parking is each lender's choice, offer by offer.
</Callout>

## Testnet and mainnet

Doji is deployed on Mezo testnet only, and its deploy script refuses mainnet. Testnet runs an older veBTC: three-word `locked()`, no veMEZO boost and no secondary voters, so there is no ValidatorsVoter. The sMUSD vault and the Router exist on testnet, and the Wave 2 pair uses both live. Doji's own [Faucet](/docs/developers/contracts#faucet) is testnet only, is separate from Mezo's faucet, and touches nothing of Mezo's but MUSD's `balanceOf` and `transfer`. Mainnet behaviour is proven by fork tests that run Doji against real mainnet positions, the real voters, MUSD, the fee distributor and the sMUSD vault, and PayDown is proven on a testnet fork against Mezo's real Router and pools. See [Run the proof yourself](/docs/developers/run-the-proof) and the [Wave 2 live proof](https://github.com/ramakrishnanhulk20/doji/blob/main/docs/proof/wave2-live-2026-11-04.md).
