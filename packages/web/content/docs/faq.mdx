## Is my veBTC safe with Doji?

Only you, the address that owned it before it entered Doji, can borrow against it or take it back, and every borrower action checks that first. A reward operator you name, such as PayDown, can only claim the position's rewards. Doji gives no approvals on veBTC, has no generic call and accepts no meta-transactions, the path behind Mezo's 5 October incident. A flaw in veBTC itself would reach every position at once, and Doji names that as a non-goal. See [Security](/docs/security).

## Do I keep my votes and rewards?

Yes. The lock keeps running, and while Doji holds the position you can still vote, claim pool fees and incentives, claim veBTC chain fees and extend the lock through LoanManager, with everything paid to you. Votes and rewards never count as collateral. See [At the same time](/docs/concepts/at-the-same-time).

## Can rewards repay my loan?

Yes, on a loan opened since 4 November 2026. On the loan's page, press "Turn on pay-down". Every week the keeper, or anyone who presses "Pay down now", claims the position's pool fees and incentives: MUSD repays the loan, BTC is sold for MUSD only as far as the debt needs and never below Mezo's oracle price less 1%, and everything else goes to you. "Turn off pay-down" stops it at any time. See [Rewards pay down the loan](/docs/concepts/rewards-pay-down).

## What if Bitcoin crashes?

If the debt rises above 65% of the collateral's value, anyone can start an auction. The price starts at the full value and falls to 70% of it over 72 hours, never below the debt while the Bitcoin still covers it, and it rises again if Bitcoin recovers. You keep everything left after the debt and the protocol's 5% penalty, and you can stop the auction by repaying in full. On a loan with pay-down on, a harvest whose rewards cover the debt stops it too. See [If a loan goes wrong](/docs/concepts/if-a-loan-goes-wrong).

## Can I repay early?

Yes, at any time, in part or in full, and anyone can pay towards your loan. Repaying early still costs the full amount owed on the date, because that is what makes the lender's return fixed. After full repayment the position stays in Doji with no loan until you withdraw it. See [Manage a loan](/docs/getting-started/manage-a-loan).

## Does my offer earn while it waits?

Yes, if you leave "Earn in sMUSD while it waits" on when you post it. The offer's MUSD sits in Mezo's sMUSD savings vault and earns its rate until a borrower takes it, and the borrower still receives plain MUSD. "Withdraw earnings" under Your offers collects what it earned, and Doji takes no share. The trade is that a parked offer's cancel needs Mezo's vault to pay out, while a plain offer's never does. See [Earn while an offer waits](/docs/concepts/earn-while-waiting).

## What is a credit unit?

A lender's claim on a date: 1 unit is 1 MUSD due on that Thursday. Units are ERC-1155 tokens whose token id is the Thursday, so every unit for a date on the same contracts is interchangeable. After the date they pay out a share of everything collected for it. See [Credit units and payouts](/docs/getting-started/credit-units).

## Can I sell my credit units before the date?

Yes, units from the Wave 2 contracts. On Lend, "Sell units" on a date's card lists them on UnitMarket at your price, at most 1 MUSD a unit, open until an expiry no later than the day before the Thursday. Buyers pay you in the same transaction, Doji takes 0.25% of the sale from you, and "Cancel listing" takes unsold units back at any time. Anyone can buy from "Credit units for sale" on the same page. Units from the 20 October contracts can still be sent to any address with "Send units", but not listed. See [Selling credit units](/docs/concepts/selling-units).

## What if nobody buys an auction?

The lenders receive the Bitcoin itself. Once the auction reaches its floor time unsold and the lock has ended, anyone can settle: Doji withdraws the lock and gives that date's lenders BTC worth the debt at the oracle price, the protocol its penalty in BTC, and the borrower the rest. Doji never sells the collateral on a DEX. See [Settle in BTC](/docs/concepts/if-a-loan-goes-wrong#settle-in-btc-when-nobody-buys).

## Who runs the keeper?

A script on GitHub Actions starts auctions, cancels recovered ones, settles unsold ones and closes dates, on both sets of contracts. On Wave 2 it also runs the weekly pay-down harvests, and every pass it checks that Mezo's sMUSD vault would hand back the offer money parked in it, failing loudly if not. It is scheduled every 30 minutes and in practice runs every two to six hours, because GitHub starts scheduled jobs late on a quiet repository. It has no special power: every call it makes is open to any wallet, and the contracts check every condition. Anyone can run it, or make the same calls from a loan's page. See [Keeper](/docs/developers/keeper).

## Can the team change the rules?

Not on your loan. The contracts cannot be upgraded, every setting the owner can change sits inside bounds written into the code, and each loan keeps the settings it opened with. The guardian can pause new offers, new loans and locks, and the extra MUSD a roll would pay out. On the Wave 2 contracts it can also stop new offers from earning in sMUSD, move every parked offer out of the vault at once, pause pay-down harvests, and pause listing and buying units; only the owner can turn those back on. It can never stop repaying, cancelling an offer, rolling to a later date, withdrawing, auctions, settling, redeeming, withdrawing offer earnings, cancelling a listing or returning an expired one. A parked offer's cancel can still wait on Mezo's vault, which Doji does not control. See [Risk settings](/docs/concepts/risk-settings).

## Why Thursdays?

Mezo's weekly epochs turn on Thursday 00:00 UTC, and veBTC locks end on the same weekly boundaries. A loan due on a Thursday lines up with the lock behind it, and one market per Thursday keeps a bad week from touching any other. See [How a loan works](/docs/concepts/how-a-loan-works).

## What does it cost?

Borrowers pay the lender's fixed rate; the protocol takes 10% of that interest as its own units. A loan that goes to auction or settles also pays a penalty of 5% of its debt, taken only out of what is left above the debt. A lender who sells credit units before the date pays 0.25% of the sale. The sMUSD yield a parked offer earns goes to its lender in full. Everything else costs only Mezo gas in BTC: in the testnet seed run a lock and borrow used about 660,000 to 710,000 gas and posting an offer about 99,000 to 121,000. See [Business model](/docs/business/business-model).

## Is it audited?

Each set of contracts had an independent review pass, separate from the build. Neither was a third-party audit firm. The 20 October 2026 review found no Critical or High issue, one Medium and three Low, and each was fixed with tests that failed before the fix. The 4 November 2026 review of the Wave 2 code found no Critical, High or Medium issue, one Low and six Informational; the Low was fixed before deployment, with three tests that failed on the reviewed code. See [Audit](/docs/security/audit).

## Does it work on mainnet?

Not yet: Doji is deployed on Mezo testnet only, and its deploy script refuses mainnet. Its fork tests run the contracts against real Mezo mainnet positions, voters, MUSD, the fee distributor and the sMUSD vault, PayDown runs on a testnet fork against Mezo's real Router and pools, and the code reads both veBTC versions. See [Run the proof yourself](/docs/developers/run-the-proof).

## What happened to the 20 October contracts?

Nothing was moved or closed. Doji's first contracts, deployed on 20 October 2026, keep serving everything opened on them: repaying, rolling, cancelling, claiming units, early payouts and redemption all work there as before, and the keeper looks after both sets. Every new offer and loan goes to the Wave 2 contracts of 4 November 2026. A loan rolls only within its own contracts, and the 20 October contracts have no earning in sMUSD, no pay-down and no units market. On Lend their offers carry a "20 Oct" tag. See [Two sets of contracts](/docs/concepts/how-a-loan-works#two-sets-of-contracts).

## Can I use a permanent lock?

Yes. A permanent lock never counts as expired, so it can back a loan on any Thursday within the 4-week term. If it is ever settled, Doji first turns it into a normal lock, which can add up to 28 days before the BTC comes out. See [If a loan goes wrong](/docs/concepts/if-a-loan-goes-wrong).

## What happens if Mezo's price feed stops?

Borrowing, rolling, starting auctions, buying and settling all wait for a fresh price, because Doji never acts on a cached one. Repaying, cancelling offers, withdrawing a position with no loan and claiming still work. A pay-down harvest that would sell BTC waits too, while one that repays with MUSD alone still runs. Earning in sMUSD and the units market never read the price. A long oracle outage is a stated non-goal. See [Security](/docs/security).
